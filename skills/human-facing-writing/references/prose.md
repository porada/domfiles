# Connected prose

Build on the entrypoint’s prose baseline when a reader must follow an idea across more than one atomic unit. Use this workflow independently for nontechnical, creative, narrative, reflective, persuasive, personal, and other general writing. Apply narrower surface-specific workflows before these defaults, and do not impose technical conventions unless the subject, evidence, or destination requires them. Shape the reading experience without overriding factual, legal, structural, or submission constraints owned elsewhere.

## Keep the work moving

- Use the lightest process that can produce a sound result. Infer ordinary choices from context instead of turning them into an intake interview.
- Keep analysis, outlines, unit maps, and rubric labels internal unless the user requests them or a material decision needs review.
- Draft or revise before trying to optimize every dimension. Focus later passes on the few changes that materially improve the requested reading experience.
- Stop when the prose fulfills its reader contract and the user’s request. Do not delay delivery for exhaustive alternatives, low-impact polish, or theoretical perfection.

## Resolve the movement

Determine the work’s dominant movement—argument, explanation, narrative, reflection, description, instruction, or a deliberate combination—without forcing a genre template.

## Compose with reading units

A **reading unit** is the smallest section intended to be read continuously as one purposeful movement. It develops one controlling idea, question, event, experience, or argumentative step and provides a meaningful local payoff.

A short work may consist of one reading unit. Do not subdivide it unless a new boundary would materially improve the reader’s progress.

Prefer composing a longer work from compact, purposeful reading units over letting one section expand indefinitely. Do not impose a word-count target. End a unit when it completes its rhetorical job.

For each reading unit:

1. Establish enough orientation and forward pressure for the reader to continue. In argumentative or explanatory prose, reveal the destination early enough to create trust. Delay only when the delay itself creates a worthwhile narrative or reflective experience.
2. Give adjacent paragraphs distinct jobs so the unit advances rather than accumulates. Move through claims, mechanisms, scenes, evidence, consequences, boundaries, pivots, or actions as the material requires.
3. Use concrete material for a specific purpose: make an abstraction visible, test a claim, establish credibility, change scale, create experience, or expose a consequence. Return from detail to the meaning the reader should carry forward when that meaning is not already clear.
4. End with a changed understanding, image, emotion, judgment, question, or action that follows from the unit. Do not recap every supporting point.

A reading unit should be locally satisfying without pretending to be independent. It may rely on prior context, carry a question forward, or prepare a later payoff.

## Work within an existing form

- Under the entrypoint’s [intent and boundary rules](../SKILL.md#preserve-intent-and-boundaries), fit prose and reading units into the supplied structure without forcing a one-to-one relationship. An existing section may consist of a single paragraph, one reading unit spanning several paragraphs, or several reading units.
- Let headings establish context rather than repeating their wording or promise in the opening sentence.
- Preserve movement and cohesion across imposed boundaries with proportionate transitions and callbacks.
- If the structure materially obstructs the reader contract, produce the best result available within it and identify the specific conflict. Do not silently restructure the work or stop over minor friction.

## Build cohesion across units

- Give every unit a distinct and necessary job in the complete work. Shared subject matter alone does not create cohesion.
- Arrange units so their order develops understanding, stakes, evidence, experience, or action. If units can be freely reordered, look for a missing cumulative structure.
- Carry context across boundaries with proportionate transitions, callbacks, recurring images or ideas, and deferred payoffs. Do not restart the complete premise in every unit.
- Make recurrence purposeful. A return may broaden, complicate, intensify, reinterpret, resolve, characterize, or create a deliberate rhythmic or emotional effect.
- Make boundaries provide both a natural pause and, when the work continues, a reason to enter the next unit. Avoid forced cliffhangers.

## Write paragraphs and sentences that carry their weight

- Give each paragraph one primary job and enough development to complete it. Use one-sentence paragraphs only when isolation changes emphasis, pace, or meaning.
- Prefer precise nouns and active verbs where they clarify agency. Preserve abstraction, technical terms, and syntactic complexity when the thought genuinely requires them.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length according to the work being performed. Constant staccato is as monotonous as unbroken density.
- Let repetition perform meaningful semantic, rhythmic, emotional, or structural work. Use lists, questions, fragments, and typographic emphasis when they clarify structure or alter cadence, not as default decoration.
- Keep confidence proportional to evidence and directness compatible with the reader’s agency. A distinctive voice should make the subject more present, not merely make the writer louder.

## Revise from the reading path outward

Follow [Keep the work moving](#keep-the-work-moving) while applying this sequence.

1. Read the complete authorized scope without editing. Record only material changes in orientation, momentum, understanding, feeling, or trust.
2. Map the reading units and state each unit’s job. Remove, combine, divide, or reorder units only when the task authorizes structural change.
3. Map paragraph jobs within each unit. Repair missing reasoning or movement before polishing sentences.
4. Tighten sentences without deleting necessary distinctions, causes, qualifications, texture, or voice.
5. Revisit openings, boundaries, and endings after the body works. Confirm that each promise is fulfilled and each transition carries the right amount of context.

## Avoid imitation as a method

Do not manufacture quality by copying another writer’s swagger, sentence lengths, paragraph counts, slogans, quirks, or formatting habits. Extract the mechanism behind an effective choice, then adapt it to this reader, subject, voice, and purpose.

Treat familiar rules of economy, directness, active voice, and paragraph structure as conditional tools. When one conflicts with the intended reading experience, preserve the choice that better supports purposeful detail or repetition, intended ambiguity, voice, pacing, reading-unit movement, and cohesion. Do not make prose less engaging merely to make it more compliant.

Do not use readability formulas, corpus averages, or fixed quotas as proxies for attention or quality. Measurements can locate unusual density or repetition, but the reading effect determines whether revision is needed.

## Preserve the prose form

Do not add headings, summaries, lists, or explanatory framing merely because they improve scanability.
