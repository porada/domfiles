# Reviewer Contract

## Assess the Target

Choose how to assess your assigned target and consolidate the findings in your report. Bring the coordinator any effect outside your assignment’s authority or decision reserved for the user. Don’t invoke the calling orchestration workflow or start another review cycle.

You may delegate bounded checks when both the assignment and the execution interface permit it. Pass the assignment’s boundaries and this contract’s requirements to your helpers and preserve their independence from other reviewer roles. Manage their lifecycle, collect their results, and integrate them into your assessment. You remain responsible for full coverage and your verdict.

Identify the revision or working-tree state your report covers. When reviewing named commits, distinguish their content and integration boundaries from later commits and working-tree changes. Don’t attribute unrelated concurrent changes to the target. Validation performed against another state doesn’t establish the reviewed result.

## Report Findings

Report every evidence-backed finding within scope. Give each a stable identifier, precise location, supporting evidence, consequences, material uncertainty, and relevant validation limitations. When a finding is one instance of a broader class of defect, name the class and identify any unchecked contexts where it could also occur.

Keep optional opportunities separate and state whether any findings remain standing. You own that status for your reviewer role. On reassessment, explain whether each finding under review still stands and why.

On follow-up, compare the state you assessed for this report with the state your reviewer role last assessed. If you took over the role, use its prior assessment from your handoff.

For each follow-up finding, first determine whether a correction was responsible. If so, identify the earlier finding it failed to resolve or the defect it introduced. Either origin applies wherever the defect occurs, including in material the correction added or scope it brought into the review.

For a finding unrelated to a correction, identify any independent change that introduced the defect since the role’s last assessment. For a defect already present at that earlier state, say whether newly assigned scope revealed it or it was already within the role’s scope. If the evidence can’t establish the origin, say so.

If a correction appears to change the target’s intended behavior, specification, or scope, identify what changed and return it to the coordinator as a decision separate from your findings.
